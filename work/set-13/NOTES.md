primitive-based helper block, and the first Astronomy documents in the programme.

**Device pass**: 14 of 16 render on an attached phone. The two 3D documents
(`AST-MOON-00003`, `AST-SS-00001`) are refused with `Unknown operation encountered 114` —

a fault in these documents. `EAR-METE-00013` needed `--no-check` for its known F-003

## What changed in the helper block

Geometry is generated by the player now rather than shipped. `ball`, `rock`, `rod`, `ring`,
`disc` and `ribbon` emit `meshPrimitive3D` ops where sets 1–12 wrote vertex and index arrays.
The two 3D documents here are 4.4 KB and 4.3 KB compiled; the equivalent hand-built spheres
and orbit rings would have been several times that.

`ribbon` uses `sweep` rather than `extrudePath` deliberately — extrudePath closes its ends by
scanline triangulation, which on a concave ring emits zero-area triangles that rasterize as
bands of garbage colour at glancing angles.

## Found while building

* **`rand(0)` is not `rand()`.** With an argument it returns the same value for every
  particle, so the whole population seeds at one point. Both particle documents had to be
  rewritten. The working idiom from set 1 is `rand()` with no argument, local coordinates, and
  a modulo wrap rather than a bounce.
* **An interactive document's rest state is what every still shows.** `EAR-METE-00013` opened
  at stage zero, where the storm cloud has no height, so the contact sheet and the gallery
  card would both have shown an empty panel. It now opens at the mature stage.
* **Orbit rings need a minor radius well above a pixel.** At 0.006 the torus aliased into
  dashes and read as broken geometry rather than as a thin line.
* **Trig phases are reduced modulo a turn** before reaching `cos`/`sin`. `continuousSec()`
  under a pinned clock is epoch-sized, and the products were large enough to be worth bounding
  even though it was not the cause of the bug being chased at the time.
